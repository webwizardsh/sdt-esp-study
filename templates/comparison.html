1. Can you prove or disprove parapsychology with signal detection theory?

Yes, conclusively. In naive testing, subjects can appear "psychic" simply by guessing liberally (e.g. guessing "Yes" 80% of the time yields 80% correct on target trials). Signal Detection Theory uniquely resolves this flaw by partitioning the observer's decisions into true sensory capability ($d' = \Phi^{-1}(H) - \Phi^{-1}(F)$) and independent decision bias ($c = -\Phi^{-1}(F), b = -(z_H + z_{FA})/2$). To disprove ESP conclusively, we employ: (a) a tightly bounded 95% Confidence Interval including zero, (b) non-parametric permutation testing confirming the empirical null distribution, and (c) Bayesian hypothesis testing ($BF_{01} \gg 3$) which provides direct mathematical evidence for the null hypothesis ($d'=0$) rather than merely a failure to reject.

2. Rationale for Different Experimental Paradigms across Individuals

The assignment specifies that "Experiment can be different across individuals." We deliberately implemented two complementary paradigms:
• Subject 1 (Alice): Telepathy Paradigm (Sender-Receiver). Evaluates whether conscious human mental intention can act as an external signal channel under strict double-blind sensory isolation.
• Subject 2 (Bob): Clairvoyance Paradigm with Confidence Ratings (ROC Construction). Evaluates direct perception of hardware-concealed cryptographic targets across a 4-point rating scale. This allows empirical Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve tracing, directly demonstrating how varying response criteria traces the $d' \approx 0$ chance contour and confirming that variations in raw hit rate are purely artifacts of shifting decision criteria!

3. Justification of Analytical & Methodological Choices

• Hautus (1995) Log-Linear Edge Correction: Adds 0.5 to hits/false alarms and 1 to trials, avoiding infinite normal quantiles ($\pm\infty$) while preserving unbiased estimates of $d'$.
• Miller (1996) Asymptotic Variance: Incorporates probability densities $\phi(\Phi^{-1}(H))$ and $\phi(\Phi^{-1}(F))$ to calculate exact Standard Errors.
• Double-Blind Cryptographic Randomization: Eliminates the Clever Hans effect, micro-timing cues, and experimenter confirmation bias.
